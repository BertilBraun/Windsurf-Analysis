\section{Lessons and Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The main lesson is not that custom machinery should replace general-purpose tracking,
but that a narrow output and asymmetric failure cost can justify a narrower system.
Conservative tracklets preserve uncertainty; sail color and camera-compensated motion
score continuations; and offline optimization chooses compatible links jointly. Two
rig keypoints separately stabilize focused-view composition.

No input-aligned held-out benchmark shows that this system is numerically superior to
the general-purpose trackers explored here. Similar sails, small targets, and
feature-poor backgrounds can weaken the cues; gaps beyond the three-second horizon
remain fragmented by design.
Measuring the near-zero identity-switch objective requires aligned evaluation and
complete-track audits. Within those limits, every component follows one contract:
preserve identity, stabilize the rider-relative view, and maintain exact frame
correspondence through preview and export.
