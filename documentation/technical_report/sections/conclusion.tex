\section{Lessons and Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This project is shaped less by the number of tracked objects than by the cost of a
wrong association.  The recordings usually contain only a handful of surfers, but
they combine small targets, long overlaps and occlusions, changing backgrounds, and
large camera pans.  A single identity switch can make an otherwise complete focused
video unusable.  The appropriate operating point is therefore deliberately
conservative: an uncertain continuation should remain fragmented or be rejected
rather than silently merge two riders.

The resulting pipeline illustrates the value of matching representations to the
task.  The sail occupies more useful pixels than the rider and often carries a
distinctive color pattern, so a compact sail-color descriptor can provide a more
relevant association cue than generic person appearance features.  Likewise, two
semantic rig keypoints are sufficient to define a rider-relative crop anchor and a
physical scale signal; a full human-pose representation is unnecessary for this
rendering objective.  These components are modest individually, but encode the
properties that remain observable in long-shot windsurfing footage.

Camera motion is a shared concern rather than an isolated stabilization step.  The
estimated background transform removes the dominant image motion before tracklet
association and also supports stabilized presentation.  Pose then solves a different
rendering problem by stabilizing the composition of the selected rider.  Treating
tracking and rendering together avoids optimizing trajectories that cannot be
delivered as visually stable, frame-consistent output.

Offline processing changes where ambiguity must be resolved.  The local pass can
form only high-confidence tracklets and defer uncertain crossings or gaps.  The
global association stage can then compare feasible continuations using observations
before and after an occlusion, while retaining start, end, fragmentation, and discard
as explicit alternatives to a weak link.  Finally, frame-indexed decoding carries
the resulting identity, pose, and stabilization metadata into browser preview and
export without losing the frame correspondence on which those results depend.

The present evidence remains specific to this project.  The repository does not yet
contain a current, input-aligned benchmark against the implemented general-purpose
trackers, so the report does not claim general or numerical superiority.  The golden
associations and retained diagnostics cover project footage and development cases;
they do not establish performance across other venues, cameras, weather, or fleets.
An aligned held-out comparison and complete-track audits would be required to measure
how often the near-zero-switch objective is met.

Several ambiguities are also intrinsic to the chosen cues.  Similar sail colors,
illumination changes, spray, and contaminated crops can weaken the appearance
descriptor.  A long complete occlusion may admit multiple equally plausible global
links even with future observations available.  Pose localization degrades when the
rig occupies too few pixels, forcing the virtual camera back towards less stable box
geometry.  Global motion estimation assumes that enough stable background texture
remains visible after foreground masking; feature-poor water, motion blur, or newly
revealed scenery during a pan can violate that assumption.

The main lesson is consequently not that custom machinery should replace
general-purpose tracking.  It is that a narrowly defined output and failure cost can
justify a narrower system: conservative local decisions, domain-specific appearance
and pose, camera-compensated motion, and non-causal global association.  For this
application, those choices make the system's assumptions and failure modes explicit
and align every stage with the identity-consistent focused video that the user
ultimately inspects.
