\section{Lessons and Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The main lesson is not that custom machinery should replace general-purpose tracking,
but that a narrow output and asymmetric failure cost can justify a narrower system.
Conservative local decisions preserve uncertainty; sail color and camera-compensated
motion score continuations; and offline optimization chooses compatible links jointly.
Two rig keypoints then provide a stable crop anchor and rig-relative scale cue, solving
the separate problem of focused-view composition.

The evidence remains specific to project footage: no input-aligned held-out benchmark
establishes numerical superiority over OC-SORT, BoT-SORT, or learned ReID. Similar
sails, spray, small targets, and changing or feature-poor backgrounds can weaken the
cues. Gaps without observations beyond the three-second horizon remain fragmented by
design, and shorter gaps can still be ambiguous. Measuring the near-zero
identity-switch objective requires the aligned benchmark and complete-track audits.

Within those limits, every component follows one contract: preserve rider identity,
derive a stable rider-relative view, and maintain exact frame correspondence through
preview and export. That output-oriented definition explains the deliberately modest,
inspectable, and domain-specific replacements.
