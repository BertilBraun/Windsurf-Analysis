\section{Evaluation Framework and Available Evidence}
\label{sec:evaluation}

The repository implements annotation and scoring tools for quantitative association
evaluation, but the current snapshot does not include a complete reproducible
benchmark dataset or results table. Complete-trajectory visual review is still
required for product acceptance. This section therefore separates the available
evaluation framework from claims that would require a fresh benchmark run.

\subsection{Golden tracklet associations}

The project includes an annotation tool for constructing ``golden'' associations from
the same detections presented to the tracker.  It first runs the conservative
preprocessor described in \cref{sec:tracking}; an annotator then assigns every
resulting tracklet either to a physical surfer identity or to the discard class.
Tracklets assigned to one identity are forbidden from occupying the same frame.  The
saved record retains the source detection coordinates and their human-assigned
identity, so competing association methods can be scored over an identical universe
of observations.

This protocol deliberately evaluates association separately from detection.  It asks
whether the system grouped the available observations correctly, not whether YOLO
found every surfer in the source video.  That separation supports faster tracker
iteration and avoids attributing a missed detection to the association algorithm.  It
also means that an association score alone is not an end-to-end tracking score;
detection omissions, interpolation quality, and the usefulness of the final crop must
be reviewed separately.

For $N$ retained detections, the evaluator considers all unordered pairs.  A pair is
positive when both detections belong to the same annotated surfer.  In this view,

\begin{align}
  P_{\mathrm{pair}} &=
    \frac{\text{correct same-identity pairs}}
         {\text{all pairs joined by the tracker}}, \\
  R_{\mathrm{pair}} &=
    \frac{\text{correct same-identity pairs}}
         {\text{all annotated same-identity pairs}}.
\end{align}

Pairwise precision exposes false merges, whereas pairwise recall exposes
fragmentation.  The scorer also reports their F1 score, the Rand index, and the
same-pair Jaccard index as compact clustering measures. Missing predicted observations can be
treated as unique singleton tracks, preventing missing data from being rewarded as
one artificial cluster.  Discard decisions are evaluated independently as a binary
classification problem.

Pairwise precision exposes the false merges that matter most here, but even a high
aggregate score can conceal one damaging switch. Results therefore need to include
absolute false-join counts and be followed by a complete-video audit through
crossings, long overlaps, occlusions, and camera pans.

\subsection{Optimization as structured engineering evidence}

The repository implements a staged optimization workflow rather than a single opaque
parameter search.  Detector confidence and non-maximum-suppression thresholds can be
tuned against labelled boxes under an explicit false-positive-per-image constraint.
Kalman process and measurement noise are fitted by minimizing one-step bounding-box
prediction error on golden trajectories after camera compensation.  Motion and
appearance mappings are then calibrated on positive fragment pairs sampled from one
identity and negative pairs sampled from different identities.  Finally, Optuna-based
search evaluates preprocessor and ILP parameters using pairwise association scores;
trial-invariant embeddings and camera transforms are cached, and videos can be
evaluated in parallel.

This sequence was valuable even where it did not produce a publication-style
benchmark.  It separated failures due to localization, motion prediction, appearance
separation, local stitching, and global linking.  Interactive threshold tools and the
golden-tracklet viewer complemented the scalar objectives by exposing the cases that
matter disproportionately to the product, especially a plausible but wrong merge.
The fitted settings are consequently best understood as engineering choices for this
camera geometry, subject scale, and collection of sails---not as universal tracker
hyperparameters.

\subsection{Qualitative failure analysis}

The most consequential failures occur when several weak cues coincide.  During a
long overlap, motion cannot separate two riders; if the sails also share similar
colors or one crop is dominated by spray and background, appearance may not resolve
the continuation either.  Rapid pans can reduce the number of background features
shared by adjacent frames, while small or blurred detections make both the Kalman
update and color descriptor less reliable.  Leaving the observations fragmented is
the intended safe response in these cases.  A false association is the more serious
failure and must be identified by inspecting the selected ILP edge and the crops on
both sides of the gap.

Other failures affect presentation rather than identity.  Missed detections create
interpolated boxes; extended gaps eventually make those boxes physically
untrustworthy.  Occluded or very small keypoints cause the focused view to fall back
to bounding-box geometry, potentially reintroducing crop drift or scale pumping.
Hard-window mining in the pose workflow ranks intervals with unstable anchors so that
these visible defects can be reviewed and added to the labelled set.  For global
motion, foreground masking reduces the risk that a large moving sail is mistaken for
the background, but it cannot create reliable correspondences when the remaining
scene is textureless or newly revealed by a pan.

\subsection{Scope of the present evidence}

The codebase contains implementations and comparison hooks for OC-SORT, BoT-SORT,
greedy association, and several ILP variants, documenting that general-purpose
alternatives were explored.  However, no current, reproducible output table compares
those baselines with the production pipeline on a held-out set.  The comparison
harness also predates the active color-stripe embedding and single-pass
\texttt{ILPTracker} interfaces, and some historical golden files require compatibility
loading after refactors.  Reporting the old runs as a current leaderboard would mix
different inputs and implementations.

The supported conclusion is therefore narrower: those experiments revealed failure
modes that motivated a domain-specific, offline design, and the present tooling makes
a controlled comparison possible after the baselines are aligned.  A release-quality
evaluation should freeze representative train/tuning and held-out videos; report
pairwise precision and recall, false-merge counts, fragmentation, discard quality,
runtime, and complete-track pass/fail rates; and stratify sequences by pan magnitude,
overlap duration, target size, and appearance ambiguity.  The design targets
reliability on this specific long-shot windsurfing task under a near-zero
identity-switch objective; measuring the improvement requires that aligned benchmark.
